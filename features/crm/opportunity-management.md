# Opportunity management in ERP.net

## Turn potential business into sales

Once a potential customer's interest develops into a genuine business opportunity, your sales team enters a new stage of the customer relationship.  

Some customers may be discussing their requirements, others may be evaluating your products and services, while others may already be negotiating commercial terms with your sales representatives.  

Understanding the potential value of these deals, their likelihood of success, and the time and effort needed to materialize them helps your business make informed commercial decisions.  

**Opportunity Management** in **ERP.net** helps your sales teams organize, evaluate, drive, and track their deals with customers while providing your business with visibility into its prospective sales and revenue.  

## What is an opportunity?

An **Opportunity**, also referred to as a **Deal** in **ERP.net**, represents a potential sale of products or services to a prospective or existing customer.

*An opportunity is the first business document claiming grounds for negotiations, meetings, and quotations with a customer.*  

While a lead represents a potential customer whose interest is being explored and qualified, an opportunity represents a specific business deal worth pursuing.  

As part of ERP.net CRM, opportunities are the first dedicated business documents for managing individual sales prospects before they become actual sales transactions. 
Their function is to bridge the gap between establishing customer interest and preparing a concrete commercial proposal.  

An opportunity may originate from objects such as:  

* A qualified lead 
* A meeting with a prospective customer 
* A practical demonstration of your products 
* A new business requirement identified within your existing customer base
* Many other activities, events, talks, or actions.  

Each opportunity represents a particular commercial prospect with its own scope, expectations, responsible salesperson, and expected outcome. 
This naturally means the same customer can have multiple opportunities.  

For example, a customer interested in purchasing new equipment and the same customer considering a separate maintenance agreement may represent two distinct opportunities, each developing at its own pace.  

## Capture and develop your business opportunities

### Understand what your customers need

The commercial details of a potential deal often become clearer as your discussions with the customer progress.  

ERP.net allows your sales representatives to capture information about an opportunity at an early stage and enrich it as they gain a better understanding of the customer's requirements.  

Depending on how far the discussions have progressed, an opportunity can contain information about:  

* The prospective or existing **customer**. 
* The **business solution** the customer is interested in.
* The **marketing campaign** from which this deal originated. 
* Preliminary **products** and **potential purchase quantities** (even if a bit early for these). 
* The anticipated **value** and **timeframe** of the deal. 
* The **sales representative** responsible for developing the opportunity. 
* **Notes** and additional information relevant to the prospective sale.  

Your salespeople can develop this information gradually as they gain a better understanding of the customer's needs, expectations, and intentions.  

This gives your representatives the flexibility to explore new business opportunities without requiring all commercial details and terms to be established upfront.
