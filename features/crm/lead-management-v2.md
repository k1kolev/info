# Lead Management in ERP.net: From First Contact to Sales Opportunity

Every new business relationship begins somewhere. It might be an inquiry from a potential customer, a conversation at an exhibition, a response to a marketing campaign, or a referral from an existing partner.

These initial interactions represent potential business opportunities. However, not every expression of interest is ready to become an actual sales deal. Before moving forward, businesses need to understand who the prospect is, what they are interested in, and whether there is a genuine opportunity to pursue.

**Lead Management in ERP.net** helps your marketing and sales teams organize this process in an action-oriented manner, turning initial interest into structured, qualified prospects that can progress toward real business opportunities.

Within ERP.net's Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system, a **Lead** is a record representing a potential customer whose interest has not yet developed into a sufficiently qualified sales opportunity.

Lead Management provides a dedicated place for gathering initial information, identifying people and companies, qualifying prospects, assigning responsibility, and preparing successful leads for the next stage of the sales process.

Unlike a sales opportunity, which represents a more concrete potential deal, a lead can initially contain incomplete, unstructured, or preliminary information. This distinction allows your team to organize incoming interest without prematurely creating formal customer or sales records, keeping the environment for actual customers and deals clean and uncluttered.

## Capture and Organize Potential Customers

When a prospect first approaches your business, the available information might be limited to a person's name, company, contact details, and a general interest in your products or services.

ERP.net allows this information to be recorded directly in a lead, providing a practical starting point for further engagement.

A lead can include:

- **Contact and company information:** The person's name, contact details, position, and associated company.
- **Marketing campaign:** The campaign responsible for generating the lead.
- **Product or solution interest:** The particular solution that attracted the prospect.
- **Business characteristics:** Information such as industry, company size, and sales area.
- **Additional notes:** Relevant details gathered during conversations or preliminary research.

This information helps your team understand where a lead originated, what the potential customer might need, and which aspects deserve further attention.

As qualification progresses, the prospect can also be identified against your existing Person and Company records in ERP.net. This supports checking for duplicates, recognizing current customers that are probing for new types and ranges of your portfolio of goods or services, and gradually organizing preliminary information into the company's established business data.

The result is a practical distinction between **collecting initial interest** and **maintaining verified business relationships**, without forcing both activities into the same place.

## Qualify Leads and Guide the Next Action

A name and an email address alone do not establish a viable sales opportunity.

Qualification is the process of learning more about the prospect, evaluating their interest, and determining whether further commercial engagement is justified.

ERP.net supports this process through a structured progression of lead stages.

| Lead stage | Business meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **New** | A lead has been recorded and awaits further processing. |
| **Qualifying** | The prospect is being evaluated and additional information is being gathered. |
| **Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL)** | The lead has reached the organization's marketing qualification criteria. |
| **Sales Qualified Lead (SQL)** | The prospect has progressed to the level of qualification appropriate for sales engagement. |
| **Closed** | Processing of the lead has concluded. |

These system stages provide a common framework for understanding the progression of leads.

ERP.net also supports **user-defined lead stages**, allowing organizations to translate their own marketing and sales practices into a structured sequence of activities. Each stage can represent a specific step in the qualification process, indicate where the prospect currently stands, and communicate the next appropriate action for moving the relationship forward.

This makes Lead Management **more than a way to record information and monitor progress**. By defining stages around their established working practices, businesses can embed their lead-handling process directly into ERP.net. Stage descriptions can provide additional guidance to employees, helping them understand what is expected at each point of the process and apply a more consistent approach to engaging prospects.

For example, a business might define stages such as *Awaiting Initial Contact*, *Clarifying Requirements*, or *Preparing Sales Discussion*. Rather than simply showing how far a lead has progressed, these stages also help the responsible team member understand what should happen next.

ERP.net additionally records when a lead first reaches the MQL and SQL stages. These timestamps provide information that can be used to evaluate qualification progress and examine how leads advance through the process.

By combining standard qualification stages with organization-specific activities, ERP.net helps businesses put their established lead management practices into everyday operation, while retaining the flexibility to adapt them to their own way of working.

## Assign Responsibility and Coordinate Sales Engagement

Once a prospect deserves further attention, someone needs to take responsibility for moving the conversation forward.

ERP.net distinguishes between the user responsible for the lead record and the salesperson assigned to its further processing. This helps businesses organize administrative ownership and commercial responsibility according to their working arrangements.

Sales representatives can be assigned directly to leads. ERP.net also supports **rule-based salesperson assignment** under specific conditions.

For example, when a lead is associated with an identified company that already has a customer record in the relevant enterprise company, configured assignment rules can determine the appropriate salesperson. This makes it possible to apply established customer-handling responsibilities when new interest comes from an existing business relationship.

The system also supports notifications associated with salesperson assignments, helping the responsible person become aware of relevant changes when the applicable conditions are met.

Together, these capabilities help sales teams establish accountability and organize follow-up around the people responsible for developing each prospect.

## Convert Qualified Leads into Sales Opportunities

Lead qualification serves a practical purpose: identifying which prospects are sufficiently promising to become actual sales opportunities.

Once a lead has been successfully processed, ERP.net supports its conversion into an **Opportunity**, also referred to as a *Deal*.

The original lead can retain a reference to the resulting opportunity, preserving the relationship between the initial prospect and the subsequent commercial process.

This transition marks an important distinction.

**Lead Management** focuses on understanding and qualifying potential customers. **Opportunity Management** focuses on developing a more concrete potential deal.

An opportunity can contain commercial information such as expected revenue, probability of success, anticipated closing date, and the salesperson responsible for managing it.

From there, the broader ERP.net CRM functionality supports further commercial activities, including preparing offers, processing sales orders, and managing invoicing.

This allows the business to advance from preliminary interest toward actual sales documentation as the commercial relationship develops.

## What Lead Management Brings to Your Business

Lead Management provides value to several people involved in developing customer relationships.

**For marketing teams**, linking leads to campaigns and recording their business characteristics makes it easier to understand the prospects generated by different marketing activities. Qualification stages also help distinguish initial inquiries from more developed interest.

**For sales representatives**, assigned leads, qualification information, and engagement stages provide context for deciding which prospects require attention and what the team's established process calls for next.

**For sales managers**, consistent qualification stages and assigned responsibilities make it easier to examine how incoming interest develops into sales opportunities, rather than treating every prospect as an equally mature deal.

Most importantly, ERP.net separates early-stage prospect management from formal sales activity while keeping the two connected through the CRM system.

## Lead Management in Practice

Consider a company participating in an industry exhibition to introduce a new product range.

During the event, its representatives meet numerous potential customers. Some express general curiosity, while others demonstrate a more specific interest in the company's solutions.

The team can register these prospects as leads in ERP.net, recording their contact details, company information, marketing campaign, and products or solutions of interest.

As conversations continue, the team can update the leads' qualification stages, identify existing company or person records, and assign appropriate sales representatives.

Using its own user-defined stages, the company can distinguish contacts awaiting an introductory call from those requiring a more detailed discussion of their needs. Team members can follow the next appropriate step according to the company's established lead-handling practices.

Prospects demonstrating sufficient commercial potential can progress through marketing and sales qualification and, when appropriate, become opportunities.

The sales team can then develop those opportunities through the relevant presales and sales processes.

Instead of treating every exhibition contact as an immediate sales deal, the company has a structured approach for identifying which relationships warrant further investment of sales effort.

## Build Stronger Sales Opportunities from the Beginning

A successful sales process does not begin with a quotation. It begins with understanding the people and businesses that may eventually become customers.

ERP.net Lead Management gives your organization the tools to capture preliminary information, organize prospect qualification, guide the next appropriate actions, establish sales responsibility, and connect qualified interest with the next stage of the commercial process.

By treating leads as a distinct part of customer relationship management, your team can develop potential relationships progressively and direct commercial attention toward prospects with meaningful business potential.
